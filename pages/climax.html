Local maximality during growth is the optimization that makes the rest viable. The naive enumerator emits a pattern at every recursion depth ≥ k — millions of redundant prefixes of their own extensions. The local rule fixes this in one line: if any extension preserves the full current membership, the longer route strictly dominates this stack — skip emission.

Emitting only when the recursion either shrinks membership or terminates cuts raw patterns by 10–100× before the global maximality filter even runs. Combined with an inverted-index dominance check (candidates restricted to patterns containing every member of B, seeded on B's rarest plate), what was an O(N²) post-process at 2.6 M patterns becomes a few seconds on tens of thousands.

What the routes revealed

A handful of hub corridors (434→404→238 and its kin) form a connected backbone that thousands of platoons share — the city's de-facto convoy arteries, invisible to a cell-by-cell view.

The size tail (platoons of 6–20 plates on a fixed route) is the signal FP-Growth could only hint at: persistent groups moving as a unit, now with directional proof.

Honest limits

The two plate counts are not a like-for-like win — MaxGrowth ran a looser prefilter (min-obs 200 over 31 days) than FP-Growth's min_count 50. The advance is qualitative: stronger evidence per pattern, not merely more patterns.

05 / The third lens · trajectory embedding

Method 3 — routes as vectors

FP-Growth scored vehicles by which bucket they shared. MaxGrowth scored them by which ordered route they co-traversed. Embedding scores them by a softer signal still: how similar their entire month-long trajectory is to another plate's. Two cars sharing a depot, two taxis working the same airport circuit, two delivery vans on the same logistics loop — none of these need to be simultaneously at the same camera. They just need their route shapes to rhyme.

t2vec
Li et al. · ICDE 2018
a sequence-to-sequence denoising autoencoder originally for GPS tracks, repurposed here for ANPR camera trajectories.
256-d
fixed embedding
a plate of 10 cameras and a plate of 3,202 cameras both compress to the same vector — the GRU's final hidden state.
0.3
--drop-prob (denoising)
30% of input tokens are dropped before encoding; the decoder must reconstruct the clean sequence — buys robustness to OCR / occlusion gaps.
cos
L2-normalized → cosine
post-train, embeddings are unit-norm; HDBSCAN clusters by cosine-like distance — two route shapes are close iff they share direction not magnitude.
WHAT FP-GROWTH CAN'T SEE
Two taxis driving the airport-loop on Mondays and the city-loop on Tuesdays — same fleet, different routes daily. No bucket they share in great numbers. Invisible to bucket-co-occurrence.
WHAT MAXGROWTH CAN'T SEE
A 200-plate corporate fleet whose vehicles cover routes similar but not identical. Each pair's d=1 gap tolerance is too tight when the shared structure is route-statistical, not route-exact. Fragmented into many tiny platoons.

The embedding asks: "What is a vehicle's route-personality, in 256 numbers?" Two plates with similar personalities are companion candidates — to be confirmed downstream by actual physical co-occurrence in the raw stream.

06 / The pipeline · three engines

Procedure — Spark · PyTorch · cuML

Hybrid by design. The data-parallel ETL stays on the existing Spark cluster (no GPU dependency, no kernel allocations); the model and the clustering move to the multi-GPU box. Three scripts, three engines, one consensus output.

stage 1 · spark clusterbuild_sequences.py

STAGE 1 · SPARK
prefilter → trajectories
275.9 M records → sort each plate by time → emit Parquet of (plate, cameras[], times[], n). Bounded by host memory thanks to --max-plates and --min-observations prefilters; the existing cluster.sbatch runs it unchanged.
output: 3,830,566 sequences
min=10 · median=38 · mean=62.5 · max=3,202 cameras
STAGE 2 · PYTORCH
denoising GRU AE
2-layer GRU encoder → 256-d hidden state → 2-layer GRU decoder reconstructs the clean sequence from a corrupted input. Per-plate embedding is the encoder's final hidden state. Trained DDP across multiple GPUs; bf16 autocast + bucket batching (32 buckets) + decoder TBPTT (chunk 256) for stability on the 3,202-token tail.
output: 3,830,566 × 256 embeddings
loss: 2.84 → 2.18 across 10 epochs
STAGE 3 · CUML
HDBSCAN + confirm
cuML HDBSCAN on L2-normalized embeddings (min_cluster_size=3) → candidate fleets. For each candidate, re-read the raw CSV and count how many (camera, ε-window) events actually contain ≥ 2 members of the cluster. Only clusters with ≥ 3 such windows are confirmed.
output: fleets.csv —
12,708 candidates · 4,041 confirmed

Why the confirmation step exists. Two commuters who drive the same road every morning for a month produce nearly identical embeddings — but they're not companions. The embedding identifies route similarity; the raw-CSV co-occurrence filter promotes it to actual companionship. This is the architectural decision that distinguishes the embedding pipeline from naive trajectory clustering.

08 / Cross-check vs. FP-Growth ground truth

What the embedding missed, what it added

We hold the FP-Growth output from Sviluppo as a high-precision oracle for small specific convoys. Checking the embedding pipeline against it surfaces a clear pattern: it doesn't replicate FP-Growth — it covers different ground.

FP-Growth findingplateswhere embeddings put themverdict
top FP-Growth pair (628× in 31 days) 477634 · 491688 both in cluster 11727 — but lumped with 206 others found, low resolution
day-1 top pair (25×/day) 323390 · 420190 split: 323390 → cluster 11541, 420190 → cluster 11477 separated
4-plate always-together set 3271142 · 16972992 · 16970573 · 511670 all noise (-1) — no cluster missed
6-car permanent convoy (~1,548 days) same 4 plus 18150551 · 3542309 all noise (-1) — no cluster missed

Why small specific convoys went noise

The denoising autoencoder embeds a plate by its route shape. Six cars that always travel together as a unit produce six near-identical trajectories — but they also produce six unique trajectories in 3.83 M-dim space. HDBSCAN's density requirement needs ≥ 3 plates within mutual-reachability of each other; six near-clones can land in a region too sparse to qualify, and tag as noise.

This is structural, not a bug. Route shape ≠ physical co-travel.

What the embedding caught that the others didn't

The 195 confirmed fleets of size > 50 — that's 195 large-scale operations FP-Growth could only express as C(N,k) bloated combinatorial blowups. MaxGrowth could capture them only as fragmented per-route platoons, never as one entity.

Cluster 11981 alone is 759 plates with 55.8 pairs/plate density. That's a real corporate fleet — and FP-Growth would have surfaced it as ~30,000 separate convoys.

The honest reading. FP-Growth, MaxGrowth and the embedding pipeline are answering three different questions on the same data: "who shares a bucket?", "who co-traverses an ordered route?", "whose route-personality rhymes with whom's?" Cross-validating across all three is how the registry in Conclusione gets built.

What the vectors revealed

4,041 confirmed companion groups across 102,779 plates — 31.8% of HDBSCAN candidates survive co-occurrence confirmation. Density grows with size: the largest fleets are also the tightest, the signature of real organised operations as opposed to lucky-coincidence groupings.

195 fleets of size > 50 — a population FP-Growth's combinatorial blowup obscured and MaxGrowth's ordered-route requirement fragmented.

Honest limits, again

The 97% noise rate isn't a failure — most of 3.83 M plates are not in any companion group, and HDBSCAN correctly leaves them alone. But the small permanent convoys (6-car set, 4-plate always-together) that FP-Growth surfaces cleanly are invisible here.

The three pipelines are complementary, not redundant. Conclusione fuses them.
