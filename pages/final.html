Cells: precise, narrow

FP-Growth's bucketed-set abstraction has the highest precision on small convoys. Its tradeoff is recall on anything larger than a handful of plates — the support-set explosion drowns the real signal in combinatorial noise.

Routes: directional, defensible

MaxGrowth turns "shared a moment" into "co-traversed a path", which is much harder to fake by coincidence. Each pattern carries a directed proof. The ε + d parameters absorb OCR/occlusion gaps the cell view can't.

Vectors: shape, breadth

The embedding moves from where you were when to what your month-long route shape was. That captures large operations whose vehicles never simultaneously co-locate but share a routing personality — invisible to the other two.

The complementarity, formally

FP-Growth and MaxGrowth share a primitive (physical co-occurrence in space-time) and differ on shape (set vs. sequence). The embedding's primitive is entirely different (route similarity), and its co-occurrence confirmation step is what keeps it grounded.

The Venn diagram is real, not rhetorical: each method's recall has a non-empty difference set, and the unions cover ground no single method covers alone.

What "confirmed across methods" means

A convoy that appears in FP-Growth output, MaxGrowth output, AND a confirmed embedding cluster is a triply-corroborated finding — three independent algorithmic witnesses to the same physical truth. These are the entries that survive the strongest possible filter.

Conversely, single-method outputs are candidates, not conclusions.

03 / Downstream · consensus fleet registry

The registry, three-tiered

The natural downstream artifact is not "the union of all three outputs" — that's just noise plus noise plus noise. It's a tiered registry where confidence is gradient: triple-witnessed at the top, single-method candidates below.

tier 1 · gold triple-witnessed fleets in FP-Growth ∩ MaxGrowth ∩ Embedding-confirmed. Highest precision; the operational core.
tier 2 · silver double-witnessed fleets two-of-three agreement. Includes large embedding fleets (MaxGrowth-fragmented but real) and small FP-Growth convoys (embedding-missed but real).
tier 3 · candidate single-method candidates surfaced by one method only. Investigative leads, not conclusions. Best for manual triage and parameter-sensitivity studies.

Applications the registry unlocks

Fleet identification. The 759-plate cluster 11981 is a real delivery / courier / ride-share operation. Pair the plate list with vehicle-class metadata to identify the operator.

Anomaly detection. A vehicle that consistently joins a known fleet's trajectory pattern but isn't a registered member — surfaceable by re-encoding new sightings and matching to confirmed fleet centroids.

Saturation mapping. Cross-reference confirmed-fleet trajectories with camera coverage; corridors where multiple gold-tier fleets share a route are critical infrastructure.

What we deliberately don't do

No individual identification. The pipeline operates on plate IDs as opaque tokens. No name, no registration lookup, no vehicle-owner inference. The registry is about group dynamics, not individual surveillance.

No predictive scoring. "X plate is likely a member of Y fleet" claims need legal review we haven't done. The registry catalogues observed patterns; it doesn't extrapolate to future behaviour.

No real-time deployment. Batch analysis over a closed 31-day window only. Streaming variants (Flink 2025) are documented possible work, not delivered work.

CALIBRATION NOTE
Tier thresholds (3-window co-occurrence, ε=300s, max-cluster-size=50, etc.) are chosen by sensitivity sweeps documented in doc/MaxGrowth.md and doc/Embeddings.md. Different downstream uses warrant different thresholds; the pipelines are parameterised, not opinionated.

04 / What the data taught us

No single lens sees this city

The most important thing we built isn't any one method's output. It's the methodological discipline of cross-witnessing: every claim about who travels with whom is only as strong as the number of independently-derived evidence streams that support it.

On methods

Three algorithms with three different primitives, each with a different failure mode, on the same dataset, produce three views with measurable overlap and measurable gaps. The gaps are not bugs — they're what each method is intrinsically blind to.

On data

275.9 M records yield 783 (FP-Growth) to 102,779 (embedding) "interesting" plates depending on the lens. The interesting plates are not a fixed property of the data — they're a property of the question you ask.

On honesty

Every method here has documented failure modes (NaN trains, NCCL crashes, OOMs, OCR ghosts, false-positive routes). The pipeline doesn't pretend they didn't happen — .changelog records each, and the doc/ tree calibrates the parameters that mitigate them.
