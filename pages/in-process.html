§1 Reproduction Substrate

The original work targeted a 5-node × 8-core × 8 GB Hadoop/YARN cluster with HDFS for shared storage. Our reproduction substrate replaces that stack end-to-end with what the platform actually provides: Slurm for container scheduling and GPFS as the shared filesystem, sidestepping HDFS and YARN entirely. Spark runs in standalone mode within the allocated containers; the cluster lives only as long as the job.

§2 Methodology

We retain the baseline's two algorithmic ideas — parallel FP-Growth on per-bucket plate sets, and half-overlapping time grids to recover convoys that straddle bucket boundaries — and drop the engineering scaffolding (iterative time-chunking, aggressive plate pre-filters) that the constrained cluster forced on the original.

Transaction Construction

For each (location, bucket) we form a transaction = the distinct set of plates passing the checkpoint during that bucket. We run this twice with two bucket origins: offset = 0 and offset = window/2. A convoy whose members cross a boundary at t = 299 / t = 301 is split by Grid A yet kept together by Grid B; taking max(freq_A, freq_B) per itemset recovers it.

Frequent-Itemset Mining

Spark MLlib's distributed Parallel FP-Growth mines frequent vehicle groups (sizes 2–6) in a single pass per grid. With min_support = min_count / |transactions|, the threshold automatically scales with data volume; the user supplies absolute min_count, the algorithm picks the relative support.

Density gate. Buckets containing more plates than --density-cap are dropped before FP-Growth sees them. Picked at 2,000 from a polars-based per-location density study — 15 % above the largest legitimate bucket (1,718) and well below the freak 23,595-plate outlier at location 31 that crashed the first run.

§4 Interpretation

The mined convoys cluster into three operationally distinct fleets, readable directly from the top-K lists above:

Fleet A — the 20×/day duo. (477634, 491688) co-occurred 628 times in 31 days, i.e. ~20 × every single day. This is not "frequent co-travel" — these two vehicles operate as a unit on a fixed schedule. Most likely interpretation: a paired commercial service (delivery duo, escort vehicle pair, or maintenance team).
Fleet B — the loose 4-car cluster. {1737487, 3279908, 4863665, 5093843} dominates the k = 3 and k = 4 leaderboards in various combinations. A group of ~4 vehicles whose pair-co-occurrence rates are high but not always synchronized — consistent with same-route commuting, not formation driving.
Fleet D — the permanent 6-car convoy. {16970573, 16972992, 18150551, 3271142, 3542309, 511670} appears at exactly the top of both the k = 5 and k = 6 leaderboards with the same members ± one. The four plates 3271142, 16972992, 16970573, 511670 each appear in 1,548 convoys — the same number — meaning they are always observed together. The most compelling actionable finding: this is a six-vehicle unit on a shared route, almost certainly an organized fleet (security escort, tow team, freight relay, or similar).

Caveats Worth Investigating

Plates 16970573 and 16972992 differ by one digit; a single OCR error could falsely couple them. The same applies to 17210840 / 17210845 (Fleet C, k = 4). A short verification pass — sampling the raw frames or plate images that generated these records — should resolve which convoys are real and which are artifacts of recognition error.

§5 Forward Work

Algorithmic Acceleration

The dominant cost in the current pipeline is FP-Growth's conditional-FP-tree construction on wide buckets. Two directions worth pursuing:

Plane-sweep within buckets. Replace the dense collect_set→FP-Growth path with a sorted sweep that emits pair candidates with sub-quadratic locality. Eliminates the stack-depth pressure that forced -Xss16m on executors.

Pre-clustered candidate sets via MinHash LSH. A single MinHash pass over plate-bucket signatures finds candidate co-traveler sets up-front, restricting FP-Growth to small bucket sub-populations and converting a global mine into many local ones.

Horizontal & Vertical Scaling

Current allocation is 5 nodes × 20 cores × 100 GB. Two readily available scale-out paths on this platform:

Wider: request --ntasks=20 containers and partition the time domain into 4 independent quarters; convoys that cross quarter boundaries are recovered by a final small merge. Linear speedup expected up to the point where shuffle bandwidth becomes the bottleneck.

Larger data, same code. Nothing in the current job is hardcoded to 31 days or 5.8 GB; a year of data (~70 GB) should run with the existing parameters in a 4–6× longer wall, modulo memory-tuned density-cap and shuffle partition counts.

Open Questions

• Spatial trajectories of Fleet D: which checkpoints, what time of day, same route every day or rotation?
• Cross-fleet adjacency: do A, B, D share any locations or are they spatially disjoint?
• OCR-noise filter: build a plate-similarity index and demote one-edit-distance pairs in the convoy ranking.
